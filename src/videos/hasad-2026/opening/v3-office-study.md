# v3 · Office environment study (before building)

Used by: 1A (wide, sunset), 1B (paper from above), 2A (night), 2B (desk from above), the time-passing montage (fixed camera, light changes from sunset → night → dawn).

## 1. Place and identity
- **The Saudi Irrigation Organization (المؤسسة العامة للري)** started as the Al-Ahsa irrigation and drainage project (completed 1971). Al-Ahsa is the oldest and largest farming oasis in the world; a 2026 international irrigation award went to its heritage irrigation system.
  → **Through the window:** the Al-Ahsa oasis is a sea of date palms (we reuse the detailed palm asset at low LOD with haze), with the silhouette of the **flat-topped Jabal Al-Qarah mesas** on the horizon and low city buildings that light up at night.
- **Saudi identity without clichés:**
  - Arabic coffee on a small tray on the corner of the desk: a brass dallah, three finjan cups, and a plate of dates. This replaces the Western coffee mugs, and in the montage the finjan cups pile up as time passes.
  - A wooden lattice (mashrabiya-style) screen on part of the window: it casts patterned light on the floor and the desk at sunset. This gives "storytelling light".
  - A pointed arch at the top of the window, a light reference to the arches of Al-Ahsa architecture.
- **Consistency with the approved storyboard (M-01A / M-02A):** window on the left, drafting desk, desk lamp, round wall clock on the right, rolled blueprints on a shelf.

## 2. Drafting desk (engineering accuracy)
- The board is at least **1.2 m wide**, tilted about 15°, on a wooden stand.
- A **parallel bar** spans the board's width on cables and pulleys (the modern replacement for the T-square). The paper is fixed with **masking tape at the corners**.
- Tools on the board and the side ledge:
  - a set of triangles (45° and 30/60°)
  - a triangular **scale ruler** (engineer's scale)
  - a compass
  - mechanical pencils and an eraser
  - an eraser shield and a drafting brush
- An **adjustable drafting stool** (ergonomics).
- An **architect lamp** (anglepoise): a conical metal shade, two arms with springs, a clamp on the edge of the board.
- Rolled blueprints in a cylindrical bin next to the desk, held with rubber bands. A wide-drawer plan chest.

## 3. Lighting in the anime-background style (lessons)
- Makoto Shinkai is known for **magic hour**: high diffusion and bloom that give a soft, "humid" atmosphere. Twilight is used to express **transition and the passage of time**, not only for beauty. This is the montage idea exactly.
- **Density of detail:** the frame is "packed with data", with small details that make the place feel lived-in. → Clutter on the desk with intent: papers, sketches pinned on the wall, a project map in a frame, books.
- **Warm/cool contrast:**
  - Sunset: gold coming through the window, violet shadows.
  - Night: the lamp's warm cone in the middle of navy blue.
  - Dawn: cool blue that slowly turns gold.
- Dust in the window's light beams and in the lamp's cone.

# Study round 2 (after the user's notes)

## Curtain (fabric)
- In a real curtain the folds come from **gravity and the gathered header**, not from a drawn wave. Professional method in Blender: a **cloth simulation** with a **pin group** on the header row (pinch pleats), then let it fall until it settles and freeze it (ref: Blender Guru, curtains tutorial — pleats, texturing, translucency).
- The fabric: medium-weight linen, cream. The bottom **hem** has weight and thickness. The **tie-back** gathers the fabric at roughly 1.2 m, so the fabric swells above and below it.
- **Backlight:** at the window the fabric glows from behind (translucency), which is the most beautiful thing about curtains in anime.

## Sadu rug
- UNESCO lists Al Sadu for Saudi Arabia and Kuwait (2020). Its patterns are **narrow bands of geometric motifs**:
  - **drus al-khail** (horse teeth: a row of triangles)
  - **al-'uwairjan** (dots from alternating warp threads)
  - "comb" stripes
- Colours: black, white, brown, beige, and red.
- Modern sizes: about 150×150 cm, or runners. Traditional pieces are modular.
- **Logical size here:** 2.2×1.5 m under the whole work area (the table and the stool together), not halfway under the table. It has **fringes (tassels)** at the two short ends and a visible 1 cm thickness.

## Book (one asset, reused)
- Parts:
  - **The case:** two boards plus the spine, made separately from the text block.
  - **Overhang:** the boards extend about 3 mm beyond the pages on three sides.
  - **The joint:** the groove between the board and the spine.
  - **Headband/tailband:** a small cloth band at the top and bottom of the spine.
  - **Text block:** a pale page block with fine lines on its edges.
- On the spine: two raised bands, a lighter title panel, and thin gold lines.
- **Reuse:** a few colour/design variants, scaled in height and thickness. Some books lean, and some are stacked horizontally.

## Jabal Al-Qarah
- A **mesa** about **75 m** high, 15 km northeast of Al-Hofuf.
- Layers (the sources differ): grey crumbling sandstone with interleaved layers of **red marl**, a cap of about **2 m of limestone**, and a reddish sedimentary base.
- The caves come from water dissolution and the collapse of the upper layers; **straight corridors** come from fissures (28 corridors, 1.5 km).
- **Anime colouring:**
  - horizontal strata bands: a reddish base, a grey-beige middle with thin red stripes, and a pale limestone cap overhanging like a ledge
  - vertical erosion grooves
  - dark cave mouths at the bottom
  - at sunset: faces lit gold and violet shadows
- **To see it from the window:** the office is on the **third floor** (about 9 m above the ground). That way the window looks over the crowns of the palms (a real "sea of palms") and the mountain shows on the horizon.

# Study round 3: why the curtain didn't read as a curtain
Problem: (1) it was the same colour as the cream wall → no contrast; (2) the simulation flattened the folds; (3) the rod had no rings, the curtain had no header, and the bottom had no hem.

Drapery anatomy (pinch pleat):
- **Fullness 2–2.5×** the track width (2.5–3× for triple pleats). **Sheers/voile need 2.5–3×.**
- Pleat spacing about **5 in ≈ 12.7 cm**, with a 2 in side return (from one supplier).
- **Bottom hem 2–4 in (5–10 cm)**: heavier, and shows as a band.
- Fabric conservation: a 2.5× width that has been gathered becomes **deep vertical tubes**. Their depth grows wherever the fabric is gathered more (at the tie-back → an hourglass shape).

The decision:
- **Two layers.** Heavy drapes in a colour that contrasts with the wall: **muted teal**, with a darker header band and hem. On top, a **white voile sheer** pulled partly across the window, semi-transparent and glowing in the backlight.
- **Folds are built procedurally** with depth tied to the amount of gathering, rather than a simulation that flattens them.
- **Rings on the rod** at every pleat, and **finials** at the two ends.
- The heavy drape **touches the floor** at its bottom.
